# Arabic pronunciation examples

## Human Qur'an and dua recordings from APIs

`recordings.js` takes priority over synthetic examples. It contains 27 complete
ayahs recited by **Mahmoud Khalil Al-Husary**, sourced using Al Quran Cloud's
`ar.husary` edition, and 5 exactly matched daily prayers recited by
**Muhammad Jumah** for **OpenDua**. The MP3s are bundled unchanged and fetched
only when played; no API request or API key is needed during normal play.
Each entry retains its source API, original Arabic, reciter, reference, URL and
SHA-256. First ayahs of short surahs include the recording's opening basmalah;
the interface says so. We do not cut verses or join qari words into new prayers.

- Al Quran Cloud API: <https://alquran.cloud/api>
- CDN integration: <https://alquran.cloud/cdn>
- Terms: <https://alquran.cloud/terms-and-conditions> (educational streaming,
  embedding and downloading; recitations remain the reciters' copyright).
- OpenDua API: <https://opendua.org/developers>
- OpenDua audio licence, **CC BY 4.0**: <https://opendua.org/licence/audio>
- CC BY 4.0 terms: <https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/>

OpenDua dataset v0.0.4 (https://opendua.org), by Ihsan Labs at the Human
Development Fund. Based on Hisn al-Muslim by Sa'id ibn Ali ibn Wahf al-Qahtani.
OpenDua editorial text, dataset structure and the recitation by Muhammad Jumah
are licensed CC BY 4.0. OpenDua makes no ownership claim over the underlying
religious texts. Audio v0.0.2. No changes were made to these recordings.

Run `python3 scripts/import_recitations.py` to regenerate the imports. Matching
uses the API's Imlai edition for ordinary lesson spelling, while preserving the
unmodified Uthmani text. It rejects multipart prayer tracks and partial verses.
**10 of 15 existing prayers have no whole, exact-text match** in this source;
they retain explicitly labelled synthetic examples. Their text is listed in
`recordings.js` under `unmatchedPrayers`. We have not silently substituted another
prayer or described synthetic speech as teacher-certified makhraj/tajwid.

## Human-recorded marked letters

`letter-*.mp3` are trimmed, mono 24 kHz MP3 conversions of the first recording
by Male 1 for each required syllable in:

Adiwijaya; UNTARI NOVIA WISESTY; Mohamad Syahrul Mubarok (2021),
**Marked Hijaiyah Letters' Pronunciation Dataset**, Telkom University Dataverse,
version 1.1. <https://doi.org/10.34820/FK2/XACWLX>

The dataset metadata states **CC0 / CC0 Waiver**. Original file names, individual
file DOI links, and licenses are retained in `manifest.js`. Downloaded WAVs
are checked against the source MD5 before conversion. Silence is trimmed; 160 ms
of trailing silence keeps adjacent syllables distinct. Pronunciation is not
generated from letter names.

Related publication: *A classification of marked hijaiyah letters' pronunciation
using hidden Markov model*, <https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4994439>.

## Synthetic vocabulary and daily prayer examples

`word-*.mp3` and `prayer-*.mp3` contain the vocabulary and all 15 daily prayers read with Microsoft
`ar-SA-HamedNeural` (Saudi Arabic), generated using `edge-tts==7.2.3` at -12%
speaking rate. They are synthetic speech, not human or Qur'an recitation recordings.
The manifest retains the exact source text, voice and rate for every file.

All vocabulary uses the same voice across devices. Pausal forms are natural at
the end of standalone words; final inflection and tanwin may not be pronounced.
Indonesian transliteration is a reading aid, not a phonetic transcription.

Audio samples were checked for intelligibility; no claim of teacher-certified
makhraj or tajwid is made. Daily prayers are spoken learning examples, including
prayers quoted from the Qur'an; they are not tajwid or tilawah recordings.
The separate Qur'an/tajwid lesson modules are not generated by this script.
Where a matching human recording exists, it overrides these older synthetic
files. Remaining vocabulary, unmatched prayers and device speech are not
reference recordings for tajwid. An imported human recording never falls back
silently to TTS: playback retries locally, then at the provider, then offers a
visible retry.

`unlock.wav` is 50 ms of generated PCM silence, used only to activate the shared
player during a tap before the next-card animation. It contains no recorded voice.

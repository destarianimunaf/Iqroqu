# Arabic pronunciation examples

## Human-recorded marked letters

`letter-*.mp3` are trimmed, mono 24 kHz MP3 conversions of the first recording
by Male 1 for each required syllable in:

Adiwijaya; UNTARI NOVIA WISESTY; Mohamad Syahrul Mubarok (2021),
**Marked Hijaiyah Letters' Pronunciation Dataset**, Telkom University Dataverse,
version 1.1. <https://doi.org/10.34820/FK2/XACWLX>

The dataset metadata states **CC0 / CC0 Waiver**. Original file names, individual
file DOI links, and licenses are retained in `manifest.js`. Downloaded WAVs
are checked against the source MD5 before conversion. Silence is trimmed; 160 ms
of trailing silence keeps adjacent syllables distinct. Pronunciation is not
generated from letter names.

Related publication: *A classification of marked hijaiyah letters' pronunciation
using hidden Markov model*, <https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4994439>.

## Synthetic vocabulary and daily prayer examples

`word-*.mp3` and `prayer-*.mp3` contain the vocabulary and all 15 daily prayers read with Microsoft
`ar-SA-HamedNeural` (Saudi Arabic), generated using `edge-tts==7.2.3` at -12%
speaking rate. They are synthetic speech, not human or Qur'an recitation recordings.
The manifest retains the exact source text, voice and rate for every file.

All vocabulary uses the same voice across devices. Pausal forms are natural at
the end of standalone words; final inflection and tanwin may not be pronounced.
Indonesian transliteration is a reading aid, not a phonetic transcription.

Audio samples were checked for intelligibility; no claim of teacher-certified
makhraj or tajwid is made. Daily prayers are spoken learning examples, including
prayers quoted from the Qur'an; they are not tajwid or tilawah recordings.
The separate Qur'an/tajwid lesson modules are not generated by this script.

`unlock.wav` is 50 ms of generated PCM silence, used only to activate the shared
player during a tap before the next-card animation. It contains no recorded voice.
